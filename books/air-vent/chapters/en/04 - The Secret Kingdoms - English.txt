My father's knife moved through the steak as easily as through butter. The meat was still bleeding a little, exactly how he liked it.

“MJ, have you stopped lying?” he asked without looking up from his plate.

“Yes, sir,” I answered. “I let my imagination go too far.”

My father smiled and pointed his knife toward my mother. “And what do you want to say to the woman who gave birth to you?”

My face turned red. “I'm sorry, Mother.”

“That's my good boy,” he said, continuing to eat. “So you've learned your lesson, haven't you?”

“Yes, sir.”

The conversation at dinner turned to the church fundraiser the following weekend. My mother promised to bake her famous pecan pie, while my father discussed which people from the Bible study group would attend. After dinner, I asked to go back to my room.

“Polly?” I whispered into the air vent. I heard a few small sniffs, as if she were still crying. Guilt filled my chest again.

“Polly, I'm sorry,” I murmured. “Can you forgive me?”

“I guess so,” Polly answered.

“Are you okay?”

“No.”

I looked around the room for a moment. “Well, when I'm sad, I like to play a game,” I explained as I picked up my toy sword. “Do you want to play with me?”

“Okay.”

“Okay, I'll be the prince, and you can be the princess. We can each have a kingdom to rule. Your kingdom will be the attic, and mine will be this bedroom. Does that sound fun?”

I heard her sniff. “Can I be a princess?”

“Of course!” I promised her. “You can be anyone you want to be.”

And that was how it all began: with a game. Every night, we waited until I heard my father go to sleep, and then we used our secret code. I tapped twice on the inside of the air vent; if Polly was able to talk, she tapped twice in return. Then we knew it was safe to play. Some nights, we ruled our kingdoms; on other nights, I read stories to her from a book. Some nights we just talked. It felt good to have her there. I slowly got used to our routine. But like every routine, sometimes it was interrupted.

Sometimes I tapped on the air vent and heard no answer, only strange groans and an occasional thud. Whenever I went downstairs, I saw my mother silently knitting in the living room, her knuckles white. My father was nowhere to be seen. That was how I knew he was in the attic with Polly. After those times, Polly usually did not want to talk much.
